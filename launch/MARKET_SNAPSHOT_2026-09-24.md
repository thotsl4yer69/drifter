**Date:** 24 September 2026

This is an internal positioning snapshot. Re-check pricing and feature claims before publishing comparisons.

### OBDLink MX+
- listed at **US$139.95**;
- high-performance Bluetooth adapter;
- broad third-party-app compatibility and OEM-specific data support on supported vehicles;
- still primarily an adapter + external app/computer workflow.

Source: https://www.obdlink.com/products/obdlink-mxp/

## Strategic implication

DRIFTER should **not** compete on cheap code-reading hardware. Established products already occupy roughly the sub-US$150 phone-adapter category with mature apps and broad compatibility.

The defensible wedge is different:

1. dedicated in-vehicle computer rather than phone accessory;
2. persistent local logging;
3. incident black box with bounded pre/post evidence;
4. explicit adapter / ECU / PID state;
5. touchscreen recovery and field workflow;
6. open Raspberry Pi/Linux architecture.

## Commercial model to test

Do not announce final retail pricing yet.

Test demand in this order:
1. free/open founding software beta for technically capable testers;
2. paid preconfigured image/support package only after onboarding is repeatable;
3. founding hardware kit only after power, display, enclosure and adapter BOM is locked;
4. assembled appliance only after support burden and failure rate are known.

A DRIFTER hardware product will naturally sit above basic Bluetooth dongle pricing because it includes compute, display/power integration and a dedicated appliance workflow. The value story therefore must be **persistent evidence + dedicated node**, not “reads more codes”.
