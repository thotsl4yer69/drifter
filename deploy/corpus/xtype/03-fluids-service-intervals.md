# X-Type fluids and service intervals

What goes in the car, how much, how often. Where figures are approximate they are flagged; when topping up after a leak, match what came out rather than trusting a single number.

## Engine oil — specification

A 5W-30 oil meeting Ford WSS-M2C913-family approvals is the correct choice for the AJ-V6; this is the same spec family as contemporary Ford petrol engines. Full synthetic 5W-30 of a reputable brand meeting that approval is standard practice among owners. Do not jump viscosities without reason — the VCT system depends on correct oil pressure and cleanliness, and old hard-driven examples with sludgy oil show it first in cam-timing behaviour.

## Engine oil — capacity and interval

Capacity is approximately 5.5 litres including the filter at a change. The factory schedule runs to a 10k-mile/12-month interval; owners keeping these cars long-term typically halve that to 5–7.5k miles or annually, which is sensible given the age of surviving engines. Always fit a quality filter. Check level regularly between services — some consumption at high mileage is normal, sudden loss is not.

## Engine coolant

Orange/pink organic acid technology (OAT) coolant meeting Jaguar specification WSS-M97B44-D, mixed 50/50 with water — the same chemistry family as Ford's orange coolant of the era. Total system capacity including heater is in the region of 7 to 8 litres. Fill to the MAX mark on the expansion tank only. Never mix with blue/green silicate coolants; if the existing charge is unknown or rusty (a cloudy brown colour was a known symptom of neglect), do a full drain-and-fill rather than topping up. Bleed properly after any opening of the system — air locks show as gauge spikes and heater failure.

## Spark plugs

Replacement interval is specified around 70k miles; plugs pulled much beyond that measure badly worn gaps (worn examples measured near 0.064 inch versus a fresh 0.050 inch / ~1.3 mm setting). Use OEM-type double-platinum or iridium plugs gapped about 1.3 mm; exotic multi-electrode designs gain nothing here. The upper intake manifold must come off, so renew the manifold O-rings/gaskets while in there and torque plugs gently — the heads are aluminium. Budget a couple of hours DIY.

## Automatic transmission fluid (Jatco JF506E)

The five-speed auto needs ATF meeting the Jaguar/Jatco specification for this box — Mercon V-type fluid is what is commonly used; using the wrong fluid destroys these transmissions. Jaguar marketed lifetime fill but specialists recommend drain-and-fill roughly every 40–60k miles. A pan drop changes only about half the total fill (roughly 4 litres drains; total capacity near 9.5 litres) because the converter and cooler hold the rest; full exchanges are done by specialists. Critical: the level is set at a specified fluid temperature with diagnostic support — never top up cold-and-guess. Pan bolts go back in gently in cross pattern.

## Transfer case / PTU oil

AWD models carry a power take-off unit needing 75W-90-type gear oil. There is no drain plug from the factory and no accessible filler on top — it sits against the oil pan. The community method: drain via the lower plug, then pump/refill through an adapter fitted at the drain port. Service every few years despite "lifetime" claims; dark or milky oil means it was overdue. A neglected PTU is one of the most expensive failures on this car.

## Rear differential oil

The rear differential takes 75W-90-type gear oil, roughly 1.2–1.5 litres. Rarely touched and rarely problematic compared with the PTU, but worth checking for seepage around the casing during underbody inspections, particularly alongside IRS bush condition.

## Brake fluid and hydraulic items

DOT 4 brake fluid, changed on a two-year cycle — these cars are old enough that moisture-saturated fluid is common and ABS units suffer for it. Bleed order works furthest-from-master-first (rear right, rear left, front right, front left). Power steering uses standard PS fluid; whining racks usually mean low fluid or a tired rack.

## Filters and consumables

Air filter at each major service (a clogged filter exaggerates lean conditions). Cabin/pollen filter yearly. Fuel filter on the petrol models when trims drift positive with stuttering under load — a restricted filter makes the pump work harder. Coolant hoses are twenty-year-old rubber: replace proactively rather than waiting for the roadside failure.

## Interval summary

Engine oil and filter: 5–10k miles / annually. Coolant: long-life OAT, inspect annually, full change if contaminated. Spark plugs: ~70k miles with manifold gaskets. ATF drain-and-fill: 40–60k miles. PTU oil: every few years. Rear diff: inspect, change if disturbed. Brake fluid: two years. Air filter: annually. This schedule reflects owner-mechanic consensus for longevity rather than the brochure minimum.
